KeroBurn beta

A free Mac app for XGecu T48 and TL866II+ chip programmers. Pick a chip, drop a file, hit Write. No Windows, no Terminal.

KeroBurn

I flash the same handful of chips over and over for our Mac parts (39SF040, 29F040, 28C64, GALs), and I got tired of booting Windows for Xgpro or looking up minipro device names in Terminal. So I wrapped minipro in a proper Mac app.

KeroBurn runs minipro, the open source driver for XGecu programmers, and ships with its own copy, so there's nothing else to install.

What it does

  • Presets. Save the chips you use all the time, with their options, an emoji icon, and even the files to flash. Pick the preset and the files are already loaded.
  • HI/LO pairs and SIMM sets. Drop two or four files and KeroBurn flashes them one after another, asking you to swap chips in between.
  • Pin check before every write. If a leg isn't touching, it stops and shows you which one in a drawing of the socket. Works on the T48 too.
  • Hex View. File and chip side by side, differences in red. It also spots shifted addresses, HI/LO mix-ups and blank chips.
  • Wrong chip detection. If the chip ID doesn't match, it tells you what the chip really is. Handy for parts with a sticker over the number.
  • Write at an address. Change just part of a ROM: give a small patch file an address, keep the rest of the chip, and only that range changes.
  • Activity log. One line per job with the file name and SHA-256, so you can check later which image went on which chip.

Requirements

Mac
macOS 12 or later, Intel or Apple Silicon
Programmer
XGecu T48 or TL866II+ (T56 and TL866A/CS through minipro)
Chips
Whatever minipro supports, over 27,000 parts

First launch

The app isn't notarized yet, so macOS stops it the first time.

  1. macOS 14 and older: right-click KeroBurn, choose Open, then Open again.
  2. macOS 15 and newer: try to open it once, then go to System Settings › Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway.
